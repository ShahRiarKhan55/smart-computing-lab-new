# Researcher Onboarding Guide

**Audience:** a researcher who has just received an invitation to join the lab's website.
**Purpose:** walk through activation, start to finish.
**Prerequisites:** an invitation link from a lab administrator.

> **Do not share your password with the lab administrator or any other person.** You are the only person who will ever know it — the administrator who invited you cannot see it, set it, or recover it.

## 1. Receiving the invitation

Your administrator will send you a link privately (email, chat, or in person) — something like `https://<site>/invite/<a long random code>`. Treat this link like a password: it alone is enough to create your account. It works **once**, and expires **7 days** after it was generated.

## 2. Opening the invitation link

Open the link in a browser. If it's valid, you'll see a short activation form greeting you by your email address. If instead you see "invalid, expired, or already used," the link can't be reused — ask your administrator for a new one (this also happens if you accidentally open an old link a second time after already activating).

## 3. Setting a personal password

Choose a password (at least 8 characters) and confirm it. This is the ONLY place you ever set it — nowhere in this process does the administrator choose it for you, see it, or ask you to tell them what it is. Submit the form.

## 4. Activation

Submitting creates your account and signs you in immediately — no separate login step needed right after activating.

## 5. Completing your profile

Go to **Account → My Profile** and fill in your name, role/title, department and a short bio (a photo URL is optional). This is what your public team page shows to visitors.

## 6. Adding research information

Once your profile exists, you can:

- Add publications, news items, and research areas
- Create or join projects and research groups (ask a project/group lead or manager to add you as a member, or create your own)
- Add lab resources and knowledge documentation related to your work

Everything you create starts as **lab-only** (visible to signed-in members, not the public) until a manager publishes it — nothing you post becomes public by accident.

## 7. Checking your account settings

On **My Profile**, the **Password** section lets you change your password any time — you'll need your current one. There is no other account setting to configure; your role and any profile category are managed by an administrator.

## 8. Logging in normally, afterward

From now on, just use **Log in** in the header with your email and the password you chose. The invitation link itself is now permanently used up and will not work again — bookmark `/login` instead, not the invitation link.

## 9. What you can edit vs. what needs an administrator

See the full table in the Researcher/Lab Member Guide. In short: your own profile, your own password, and your own content, always. Your account role, deletion, and another member's content need a manager or admin.

## 10. Password & security best practices

- Choose a password you don't reuse elsewhere.
- Never share it with anyone, including your lab administrator — they will never legitimately ask you for it.
- If you think your account may be compromised, tell your administrator — they can deactivate the account; you'll need a fresh invitation to reactivate.
- There is currently no self-service "forgot password" option if you're logged out — contact your administrator.
